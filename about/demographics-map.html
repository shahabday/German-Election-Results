Demographics & Economy — Map Visualization

Census, income, and labor-market data on the same map framework as the election tools — so the "why" behind a political trend is one tab away.

Demographics & Economy Map

Zensus 2022 census data at municipality resolution (age structure, migration background, housing, household size, rent), plus county-level income, GDP, and unemployment (VGRdL/INKAR) as a multi-year time series going back to the 1990s. Every metric has a one-sentence explanation built right into the panel, so you don't need outside context to read the map correctly.

Two scale modes matter here: the default "this year" scale re-stretches its color range to whatever year you're viewing, which is great for seeing relative differences within one year but makes it impossible to compare absolute levels across years (a lighter shade in 2022 doesn't necessarily mean "lower" than a darker shade in 1995). A "fixed" scale mode locks the color range to the entire dataset's range instead, so you can genuinely track, say, the former East catching up to the West in GDP over three decades. A separate year-over-year growth view (also with a fixed-vs-dynamic choice) shows the pp change itself, rather than the absolute level.
