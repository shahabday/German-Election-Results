Demographics

Population

residents
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Total resident population at the 2022 census. The baseline denominator behind every share metric in this dataset, and useful on its own for spotting which places are growing vs. shrinking. Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census), municipality resolution.

#m-demographics-population_census22

Under 18

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents younger than 18. A rough proxy for local demand on schools and childcare, and for how much of a place's future population growth will come from residents already living there. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_under18_census22

Age 18-29

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents aged 18 to 29. Tends to run high in university towns and career-starter hubs, and low in retirement-heavy or rural areas young adults leave for education or work. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_18to29_census22

Age 30-49

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents aged 30 to 49 - roughly the family-formation and peak-earning years, so this tracks how many prime-working-age households a place has, not just its population size. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_30to49_census22

Age 50-59

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents aged 50 to 59, the pre-retirement cohort. A rising share here is a leading indicator of where the 60+ share (below) is headed next. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_50to59_census22

Age 60+

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents aged 60 and older. High values signal aging/retirement pressure on local services, and tend to correlate with lower population turnover than younger, more mobile areas. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_60plus_census22

Foreign nationals

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents who are not German citizens. This is citizenship-based only - a person born in Germany to immigrant parents, or a naturalized citizen, is NOT counted here even though they would be under migration background (below). Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_foreign_census22

Migration background

%
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residents with a migration background - includes naturalized citizens and their children, so it's broader than (and always ≥) foreign-national share. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_migration_bg_census22

Avg. household size

persons
Category: Demographics
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Average number of people living in the same household. Smaller households mean more housing units are needed per resident, and a falling average over time is often an early sign of an aging or increasingly single-person population. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-avg_household_size_census22

Housing

Single/two-family homes

%
Category: Housing
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of residential buildings that are single- or two-family houses, rather than larger apartment blocks - a rough density/urbanization proxy. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_single_family_census22

Total dwellings

count
Category: Housing
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Total number of housing units (not people). A raw stock size, not a rate - compare across similarly-sized places rather than reading it directly against a much larger or smaller municipality. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-total_dwellings_census22

Vacancy rate

%
Category: Housing
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of dwellings standing empty. High vacancy can mean a shrinking or unattractive local housing market, but can also just reflect a lot of second homes or units mid-renovation - read it alongside the population trend before assuming decline. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-vacancy_rate_census22

Owner-occupied

%
Category: Housing
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Share of dwellings lived in by their owner, as opposed to rented out. Renter-heavy places tend to see more population turnover and are more directly exposed to swings in asking rents than owner-heavy ones. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-share_owner_occupied_census22

Avg. rent

€/m²
Category: Housing
Source: Zensus 2022 (German federal census)

Average asking rent per square meter, cold (excluding utilities/heating). This is a snapshot of what's currently being advertised, not what sitting tenants pay on older leases - in fast-growing cities it can run well above the typical rent actually being paid across all existing tenancies. Source: Zensus 2022.

#m-demographics-avg_rent_per_m2_census22

County (multi-year, 1995–2022 depending on metric)

Business

Micro enterprises

%
Category: Business
Years covered: 2006–2022 (17 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "share_micro_enterprises"

Share of local enterprises with fewer than 10 employees. A high share isn't inherently a bad sign - it's the norm in tourism-, retail-, and craft-heavy local economies, quite different from a share driven by economic distress.

#m-demographics-share_micro_enterprises

Large enterprises

%
Category: Business
Years covered: 2006–2022 (17 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "share_large_enterprises"

Share of local enterprises with 250 or more employees. A county can score low here and still be prosperous if its economy runs on many strong small/medium firms rather than a few big employers - classic Mittelstand-heavy regions often look this way.

#m-demographics-share_large_enterprises

Business insolvencies (INKAR)

per 10,000 enterprises (unconfirmed unit, see README)
Category: Business
Years covered: 2007–2020 (14 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Unternehmensinsolvenzen_inkar"

Corporate insolvencies - INKAR convention is typically per 10,000 enterprises, but this hasn't been confirmed against a codebook (same caveat as median_income), so treat the exact unit with caution. Best read as a multi-year trend (rising vs. falling) rather than compared as a precise single-year level.

#m-demographics-business_insolvencies

Childcare

Childcare rate (under 3)

%
Category: Childcare
Years covered: 2006–2020 (15 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "childcare_under3"

Share of children under 3 enrolled in daycare (Kita/Tagespflege). A strong predictor of maternal labor-force participation locally - where availability is low, one parent (usually the mother) tends to stay out of paid work longer.

#m-demographics-childcare_under3

Childcare rate (3-6)

%
Category: Childcare
Years covered: 2006–2020 (15 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "childcare_3to6"

Share of children aged 3-6 enrolled in daycare. Coverage here runs much higher nationwide than under-3 care, since most German states guarantee a Kita place from age 3 onward - so the gaps BETWEEN counties are more informative than the level of any one county.

#m-demographics-childcare_3to6

Cost of living

Rent price

€/m²
Category: Cost of living
Years covered: 2010–2021 (12 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Mietpreise_inkar"

Average rent per square meter (INKAR source - compare with the municipality-level Zensus rent figure, which may differ slightly in methodology).

#m-demographics-rent_price

Over-indebtedness rate (Schuldnerquote)

%
Category: Cost of living
Years covered: 2004–2019 (16 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Schuldnerquote_inkar"

Share of adults with unmanageable debt (more liabilities than they can realistically repay) - a financial-distress indicator distinct from income level: a county can have modest but stable income and low debt distress, or higher income with more households overextended.

#m-demographics-debtor_quota

Healthcare

Physicians

per 10,000 residents
Category: Healthcare
Years covered: 2015–2022 (8 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "physician_density"

All physicians (all specialties) per 10,000 residents, regardless of whether they practice at a hospital, private clinic, or elsewhere. A county with a large specialist hospital can score high here even if everyday primary care is thin - see general practitioners below for that specifically.

#m-demographics-physician_density

General practitioners

per 10,000 residents
Category: Healthcare
Years covered: 2015–2022 (8 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "gp_density"

General practitioners (Hausaerzte) specifically, per 10,000 residents - a primary-care access measure distinct from physician density overall, and generally the better indicator of how easy it is for an ordinary resident to see a doctor nearby.

#m-demographics-gp_density

Hospital beds

per 1,000 residents
Category: Healthcare
Years covered: 2004–2021 (18 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "hospital_beds_per_capita"

Inpatient hospital beds per 1,000 residents. Reflects local inpatient capacity, not quality of care or travel distance - a rural county can show zero beds and still be well served by a hospital just across the county line.

#m-demographics-hospital_beds_per_capita

Housing

Building permits

per 10,000 residents
Category: Housing
Years covered: 1995–2022 (28 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "building_permits_per_capita"

New residential building permits issued per 10,000 residents that year - a leading indicator of housing construction, not completions.

#m-demographics-building_permits_per_capita

Living space per capita

m²
Category: Housing
Years covered: 2011–2022 (12 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "living_space_per_capita"

Average residential floor space per resident. This rises mechanically as household size falls, even with zero new construction - read it alongside average household size (municipality view) before treating a high value as pure housing abundance.

#m-demographics-living_space_per_capita

Municipal debt per capita

€
Category: Housing
Years covered: 2010–2019 (10 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "municipal_debt_per_capita"

Local government debt per resident - a fiscal-strain measure at the municipal level, distinct from tax capacity.

#m-demographics-municipal_debt_per_capita

Income & Economy

Avg. gross wage

€/year
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 2001–2021 (21 years with data)
Source: Regional accounts (VGRdL), via processed/covariates/county_gross_income.csv, column "gross_avg_wage_vgrdl"

Average gross wage per employee, from official regional accounts (VGRdL) - before tax, and averaged across full- and part-time work. This is a workplace-based figure (where people work, not where they live), so in commuter-heavy counties it can diverge noticeably from what residents actually take home - compare against household income below.

#m-demographics-gross_wage

Avg. household income

€/year
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 2001–2021 (21 years with data)
Source: Regional accounts (VGRdL), via processed/covariates/county_household_income.csv, column "hh_income_vgrdl"

Average disposable household income (after taxes and transfers), from official regional accounts (VGRdL). Unlike gross wage or GDP, this is what actually funds local consumption - it isn't inflated by commuters' workplace earnings or by capital-intensive industry that employs relatively few local residents.

#m-demographics-hh_income

Median income (INKAR)

€ (period as reported by INKAR)
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 2014–2020 (7 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Medianeinkommen_inkar"

The middle income value (half earn more, half less) - less skewed by very high earners than an average, so it's generally a better read on a "typical" resident's income than the average figures above. Exact income concept/period not confirmed against a codebook, see README.

#m-demographics-median_income

GDP per capita

€ thousand
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 2000–2020 (21 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Bruttoinlandsprodukt_je_Einwohner_inkar"

Total economic output produced in the county, divided by its population - a measure of local economic activity, not resident income (commuters can inflate it).

#m-demographics-gdp_percap

Tax capacity (Steuerkraft)

€ per capita
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 1995–2019 (25 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Steuerkraft_inkar"

A municipality's own-source tax revenue capacity per resident - used in Germany's fiscal equalization system between richer and poorer areas.

#m-demographics-tax_capacity

Purchasing power (Kaufkraft)

€ per capita
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 2013–2021 (9 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "purchasing_power"

GfK-style purchasing power per resident - a broader spending-capacity measure than disposable income alone, since it's built to capture what households actually have available to spend, not just wages before taxes and transfers.

#m-demographics-purchasing_power

Low-income households

%
Category: Income & Economy
Years covered: 2013–2021 (9 years with data)
Source: INKAR/Zensus-derived, via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "share_low_income_hh"

Share of households with income below 60% of the median - the standard EU at-risk-of-poverty threshold. Because it's relative to that year's OWN median, it stays meaningful even as overall income levels rise over time, unlike a fixed euro poverty line would.

#m-demographics-share_low_income_hh

Knowledge Economy

Expert-level jobs (INKAR)

% (unconfirmed unit, see README)
Category: Knowledge Economy

Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Beschäftigte_mit_Anforderungsniveau_Experte_inkar"

Share of employees whose job is classified at the highest INKAR skill-requirement tier (Experte) - a proxy for high-skill local labor demand.

#m-demographics-expert_jobs_share

% (unconfirmed unit, see README)

Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Beschäftigte_in_wissensintensiven_Industrien_inkar"

Share of employment in INKAR-classified knowledge-intensive industries. Tends to cluster around universities and major metro areas - a useful counterpoint to industry_share (Labor market) when distinguishing "old" from "new" economy counties.

Creative industries jobs (INKAR)

% (unconfirmed unit, see README)
Category: Knowledge Economy
Years covered: 2008–2020 (13 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Beschäftigte_in_Kreativbranchen_inkar"

Share of employment in INKAR-classified creative industries (Kreativbranchen). Usually concentrated in large cities - values much above roughly 5% are rare outside of them.

#m-demographics-creative_industry_share

Craft trades jobs (Handwerk, INKAR)

% (unconfirmed unit, see README)
Category: Knowledge Economy
Years covered: 2010–2018 (9 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Beschäftigte_im_Handwerk_inkar"

Share of employment in craft trades (Handwerk) - a traditional-economy counterpoint to the knowledge-economy metrics above, and often strongest in exactly the mid-sized towns and rural counties where the others are weakest.

Labor market

Unemployment rate

%
Category: Labor market
Years covered: 1998–2020 (23 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_additional.csv, column "Arbeitslosenquote_inkar"

Share of the civilian labor force registered as unemployed. The standard headline labor-market number, but it doesn't distinguish short-term frictional unemployment from entrenched long-term joblessness - the Society & Culture map's welfare-receipt metric captures a related but distinct dimension of that.

#m-demographics-unemployment_rate

Industry employment share

%
Category: Labor market
Years covered: 2008–2020 (13 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Industriequote_inkar"

Share of employment in manufacturing/industry, as opposed to services or agriculture - a deindustrialization proxy when tracked over time.

#m-demographics-industry_share

Commuter balance

per 1,000 residents (+ = net inflow)
Category: Labor market
Years covered: 1997–2020 (24 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Pendlersaldo_inkar"

Net commuters per 1,000 residents. Positive means more people commute IN for work than commute out (a job center); negative means the reverse (a bedroom community).

#m-demographics-commuter_balance

Migration & Diversity

Foreign national share

%
Category: Migration & Diversity
Years covered: 1995–2020 (26 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Ausländeranteil_inkar"

Share of residents who are not German citizens (county-level, INKAR source - compare with the municipality-level Zensus figure, which may differ slightly in methodology).

#m-demographics-foreign_share

In-migration rate

per 1,000 residents
Category: Migration & Diversity
Years covered: 1995–2020 (26 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_additional.csv, column "Zuzugsrate_inkar"

People moving INTO the county per 1,000 residents, per year (from elsewhere in Germany or abroad). This is gross inflow only - a county can score high here and still be shrinking overall if out-migration (below) is even higher.

#m-demographics-migration_in_rate

Out-migration rate

per 1,000 residents
Category: Migration & Diversity
Years covered: 1995–2020 (26 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_additional.csv, column "Fortzugsrate_inkar"

People moving OUT of the county per 1,000 residents, per year. High out-migration paired with low in-migration is the classic signature of a declining rural county losing working-age residents.

#m-demographics-migration_out_rate

Asylum seekers

per 1,000 residents
Category: Migration & Diversity
Years covered: 2010–2020 (11 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_additional.csv, column "Asylbewerber_inkar"

Asylum seekers registered in the county, per 1,000 residents. Reflects where asylum seekers are officially allocated/housed under Germany's state-quota distribution system (Koenigsteiner Schluessel) as much as it reflects where people ultimately choose to settle long-term.

#m-demographics-asylum_seeker_share

Public Finances

Trade tax revenue (Gewerbesteuer, INKAR)

€ per capita (unconfirmed unit, see README)
Category: Public Finances
Years covered: 1995–2020 (26 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_inkar_indicators.csv, column "Gewerbesteuer_inkar"

Local trade tax revenue - likely € per capita given the value range, but not confirmed against a codebook. Negative values in some county-years reflect real refunds/corrections, not a data error. #m-demographics-trade_tax_revenue

Municipal staff (Personal der Kommunen, INKAR)

per 10,000 residents (unconfirmed unit, see README)

Local government employment - likely per 10,000 residents given the value range, but not confirmed against a codebook. More staff isn't automatically "better government" - it can also reflect a more fragmented municipal structure (many small Gemeinden each running their own administration) rather than service quality.

Crime & Safety

Crime rate

cases per 100,000 residents
Category: Crime & Safety
Years covered: 2020–2024 (5 years with data)
Source: BKA Polizeiliche Kriminalstatistik, T01 Grundtabelle "Kreise" (bka.de)

All recorded criminal offenses per 100,000 residents that year (Haeufigkeitszahl, BKA Polizeiliche Kriminalstatistik) - reported crime, not necessarily actual crime, and shaped by local policing/reporting practices as well as by the underlying offense rate. Also pulled up mechanically by non-resident activity: a county with a major train station, shopping district, or airport can show a high rate driven by people who don't live there and therefore aren't in the population denominator.

#m-society-crime_rate

Economic Structure

Agriculture employment share

% of gross value added
Category: Economic Structure
Years covered: 2000–2021 (22 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "share_primary_sector"

Share of the county's economic output (gross value added) from the primary sector - agriculture, forestry, fishing (INKAR indicator, reused from this project's map-demographics data pull). Nationwide this share is now small almost everywhere, so even a county that looks "high" here is usually just less far along the same long-run shift out of agriculture as the rest of the country.

#m-society-share_primary_sector

Industry employment share

% of gross value added
Category: Economic Structure
Years covered: 2000–2021 (22 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "share_secondary_sector"

Share of the county's economic output (gross value added) from the secondary sector - manufacturing and construction (INKAR indicator, reused from this project's map-demographics data pull). Tracking this over time is the most direct way to see (de)industrialization happening in a county, rather than inferring it indirectly from the unemployment rate.

#m-society-share_secondary_sector

Services employment share

% of gross value added
Category: Economic Structure
Years covered: 2000–2021 (22 years with data)
Source: INKAR (BBSR), via processed/covariates/county_covariates_bonus.csv, column "share_tertiary_sector"

Share of the county's economic output (gross value added) from the tertiary sector - services (INKAR indicator, reused from this project's map-demographics data pull). Services is now the largest sector almost everywhere in Germany, so the more informative read is usually which OTHER sector is unusually large in a given county, not how large tertiary itself is.

#m-society-share_tertiary_sector

Education

School leavers with Abitur

%
Category: Education
Years covered: 2006–2024 (19 years with data)
Source: Regionalatlas Deutschland (regionalstatistik.de), table AI003-2

Share of that year's school leavers (all school types) who left with a general or subject-specific higher education entrance qualification (allgem./fachgeb. Hochschulreife) - the top academic track. Strongly shaped by whether a county has its own Gymnasium (academic-track school) or older students commute elsewhere, so this partly reflects local school infrastructure, not just household background.

#m-society-edu_abitur

School leavers without a degree

%
Category: Education
Years covered: 2006–2024 (19 years with data)
Source: Regionalatlas Deutschland (regionalstatistik.de), table AI003-2

Share of that year's school leavers who left general education school without even the basic Hauptschulabschluss - the standard proxy for being at risk of not finding an apprenticeship or job. Widely used as an early-warning indicator: counties with a persistently high share here tend to show elevated youth unemployment and welfare receipt a few years later.

#m-society-edu_no_hauptschulabschluss

Family & Marriage

Marriage rate

per 1,000 residents
Category: Family & Marriage
Years covered: 2018–2025 (8 years with data)
Source: regionalstatistik.de, table 12611-01-02-4

New marriages per 1,000 residents in the county that year (Statistik der Eheschliessungen, marriages counted where at least one spouse lives in the county, divided by county population). Marriage rates have been declining nationwide for decades as cohabitation and later marriage become more common, so a county's level relative to OTHERS in the same year is more informative than its level in isolation.

#m-society-marriage_rate

Divorce rate

per 1,000 residents
Category: Family & Marriage
Years covered: 2018–2024 (7 years with data)
Source: regionalstatistik.de, table 12631-01-02-4

Divorces finalized per 1,000 residents that year (Statistik rechtskraeftiger Urteile in Ehesachen). A single year's jump doesn't always mean more marriages are breaking down - court processing backlogs can shift when a divorce is finalized relative to when it was filed, so the multi-year trend is more reliable than any one year.

#m-society-divorce_rate

Labor & Welfare

Welfare receipt (Buergergeld)

% of population
Category: Labor & Welfare
Years covered: 2018–2024 (4 years with data)
Source: regionalstatistik.de, table 22811-02-02-4

Share of the county's population receiving basic income support under SGB II (Buergergeld/Hartz IV - Personen in Bedarfsgemeinschaften), computed here from regionalstatistik.de person counts divided by county population. This is a structural indicator, not a cyclical one - in counties where it's persistently high, it reflects the underlying local job market over years, not a recent news-cycle event.

#m-society-welfare_rate

Religion & Community

Church membership

% of population
Category: Religion & Community
Years covered: 2001–2022 (22 years with data)
Source: fowid.de, "Bundeslaender: Kirchenmitglieder, 2001-2022" (EKD + Deutsche Bischofskonferenz statistics)

Combined Protestant (EKD) + Catholic church membership as a share of the state's population (source: fowid.de, based on official EKD/Catholic diocese statistics). STATE-LEVEL ONLY - every county in a state shows the same value, since no county-level source exists for this. Membership has fallen nationwide for decades (formal church-leaving, Kirchenaustritt, is common and easy), so even a state near the top of this ranking is almost certainly still trending downward - use the year slider to see direction, not just level.
