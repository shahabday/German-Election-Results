About This Project

Germany in Maps is an independent, open-data project that visualizes how Germany's political, economic, and demographic landscape has shifted over the past nearly three decades — from federal, European, state, and municipal elections down to census, income, and labor-market data.

Why this exists

Germany's election and demographic data is public, but it's scattered across dozens of official sources in dozens of formats, and rarely presented in a way that lets you actually feel the shape of the change over time — where the AfD's rise really started, how the former East and West have (and haven't) converged economically, which places have quietly flipped from one party to another. This project gathers that data into one place and turns it into nine interactive tools, split into two themes — political and demographic/economic — each explorable as either an absolute map or a trend line.

How it's built

Every tool is a fully self-contained static page: real GeoJSON boundaries, real per-year election and demographic results, and plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript with Leaflet for the maps. There's no backend and no database — each tool loads its own pre-built JSON files directly. That keeps everything fast, inspectable, and easy to keep running indefinitely.

A couple of methodology choices worth knowing about, since they apply across every tool:

  • State and county lines are simple, unweighted averages of their municipalities — a small rural municipality counts exactly as much as a big city within it, since there's no consistent multi-year population-weighting dataset covering this whole period.
  • A colored area's "winner" is a plurality/ecological statistic, not always a real single-winner seat outcome — except where a tool is explicitly showing genuine direct-mandate constituencies (Bundestag Wahlkreise, Berlin's 2026 Abgeordnetenhaus Wahlkreise).
  • Municipality boundaries and codes (AGS) change over time as places merge or split; a handful of areas can lag or look slightly inconsistent across years because of this administrative churn, not a data error.

Data sources

  • Elections: GERDA (Heddesheimer, Hilbig, Sichart & Wiedemann, 2025, Nature Scientific Data) for the historical federal/European/state/municipal results, plus official state sources scraped directly for the 2026 Landtag and Kommunalwahl results (including Berlin's real 78-constituency breakdown) as soon as they were available.
  • Demographics: Zensus 2022 (German census) at municipality resolution.
  • Economy & labor market: VGRdL (Volkswirtschaftliche Gesamtrechnungen der Länder) and INKAR, at county resolution, as a multi-year time series back to the 1990s.
  • Boundaries: BKG VG250 and the m-ad/geofeatures-ags-germany project.
