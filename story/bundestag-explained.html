How to Read a Bundestag Map

Nine tools, one shared language. Before diving into any single story, here's what these maps actually show — using nothing but real Bundestag (federal) election results, compared between 2021 and 2025.

↓ Scroll to begin
The basic map

One seat, one color

Germany elects 299 constituencies directly — the Erststimme, or first vote. Whoever gets the most votes in a constituency wins it outright, winner-take-all. Color here is simply that winner's party. This is the real 2025 result, by state.

Party growth

What "growth" means on a map

A single map only shows one moment. Compare the same area across two elections and you get a swing — this year's share minus last time's, in percentage points (pp). Here's the AfD's growth from 2021 to 2025: +13.1pp nationally on average, peaking at +36.5pp in one small municipality alone.

Flipped areas

When the winner changes

An area "flips" when a different party wins it than last time — regardless of how big the swing was. Between 2021 and 2025, 4,477 of Germany's ~10,400 municipalities — 43% — flipped to a new winner. Most went to the CDU/CSU (2,875); the AfD picked up 1,594.

At risk

Reading the next election, early

"At risk" isn't about who's ahead — it's about who's catching up. For every area, we track its fastest-rising challenger and ask: if that same rate of growth repeats next time, would it overtake the leader? 2,487 municipalities — 24% — are flagged at risk right now. In 96% of them, the rising challenger is the AfD.

Turnout

The map isn't just about who wins

Turnout change matters just as much as vote share. 2025 brought Germany's highest Bundestag turnout since reunification — 82.5% nationally, up an average of +5.6pp, and rising in almost every part of the country.

Now you know the language

Growth. Flips. Risk. Turnout.
