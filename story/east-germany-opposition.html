The East: One Region, One Opposition

The pattern is unmistakable: CDU has been falling for decades. But who's been challenging it? This story averages voting data across five eastern states, Sachsen-Anhalt, Thüringen, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Brandenburg and Berlin, to show who used to challenge CDU, and what changed since.

↓ Scroll to begin
Five states, one region

CDU led, but never overwhelmingly

Across these five states and 36 years, CDU's regional average never exceeded 46.6%, and fell as low as 18.6% by 2021. That's a weaker, less stable lead than CDU held almost anywhere else in Germany.

SPD's regional role

SPD: Sometimes Challenging, Always Declining

SPD's regional average peaked at 40.3% in 2002, but has been sliding for most of the period since, never mounting a lasting challenge to CDU.

The real regional opposition

Die Linke, the East's actual rival to CDU

Add Die Linke (and predecessor PDS) and the pattern is clear: it averaged above 20% in every election from 2005 through 2021, peaking at 26.9% in 2009. This was the East's real opposition to CDU.

2013: the region's new opposition

AfD's regional breakthrough

AfD's regional average jumped from 5.1% in 2013 to 19.8% in 2017, and by 2025 reached 35.6%, more than triple Die Linke's best year.

Berlin's effect on the average

Why the Greens show up here at all

The Greens' regional average (5.6% in 2021) is almost entirely Berlin's doing. Pooling Berlin in with the other four East states is what makes this the one region in this series where Greens register at all.

The economic picture, regionally

The whole region converged. The whole region swung.

Household income across the region rose steadily relative to the national average between the early 2000s and 2019, though it never caught up to the West's own level. None of that convergence slowed the region's AfD growth, which climbed right alongside it.

Economic output, regionally

GDP Per Capita Grew, But So Did AfD

GDP per capita across the region nearly doubled between 2000 and 2020, though it never closed the gap with the West's own steady rise. Growth and the AfD's rise happened at the same time, not in opposite directions.

Inequality, regionally

Inequality Fell, Then Rose, East and West Alike

After a drop in 2022, the region's Gini coefficient has risen every year since, and the West shows the same pattern. Data before 2021 isn't available, so we can't say whether this is a new trend or a return to an old one.

Demographics, regionally

An Aging Region

The region's average age has crept up every year on record, and has stayed about a year and a half older than the West throughout. An older electorate hasn't meant a more moderate one.

Crime & safety, regionally

Crime Is Higher in the East

The region's crime rate has run well above the West's every year since 2020, and the gap has only grown. It's one more line moving in the same direction as the AfD's vote share.

Migration, regionally

Still the Region With the Fewest Foreign Residents

The foreign national share of the region's population has more than doubled since the 1990s, but from a very low base. It remains well below the West's, though the gap has narrowed somewhat over time.

One region, one clear shift

Five states, the same turning point

Whatever the state-by-state differences in degree, the shape is the same across the East: CDU's leading challenger was Die Linke until 2013, and has been the AfD ever since. This chart averages each state's own composite equally, not by municipality count, so no single state dominates the regional picture.
