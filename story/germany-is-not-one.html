A Germany in Maps story

Germany is Not One

Thirty-five years after reunification, one map can still tell two stories: what has faded since 1990, and what never converged at all.

↓ Scroll to begin
Demographics

The East is aging faster than the West

The share of residents 65 and older has been higher in the East since at least 2011, and that gap has nearly doubled since then, from 3.2 points to 5.6 points by 2025. Some eastern counties are now close to a third senior.

Demographics

Where Are the Young People?

In 2011 the East and West had almost the same share of 28-to-35-year-olds. By 2025 that gap has become the widest of any age group in this story.

Demographics

Average Age

Averaging every age group into a single number, the pattern is unmistakable: the average resident of the East is now about three years older than in the West, 48.3 versus 45.0 in 2025, and the gap has widened every year since 2011.

Wellbeing

Self-Reported Life Satisfaction

Self-reported life satisfaction sits in a tight band nationwide, roughly 6 to 7.4 out of 10. Hamburg usually tops the ranking. Every single state dropped together in 2021, the pandemic year, then recovered by 2024, a rare case of the whole country moving in lockstep.

Health

Living Long

Every state sits between 79.2 and 82.4 years of life expectancy at birth in 2023, the only year available, a narrow band by international standards. Baden-Württemberg and Bavaria lead; Saxony-Anhalt trails. Notably, Saxony is the one eastern state right up near the top.

Crime & Safety

Crime Rate Is Lowest in the South

The lowest-crime counties in the country are almost all in Bavaria. The East runs hotter, and so do big cities everywhere.

Labor & Welfare

Who receives Bürgergeld

Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg sit lowest on basic income support. Bremen and Hamburg run far higher, and most of the East sits in between, consistently above the southern states.

Health

Better access, everywhere, for twenty years

Health-sector employment per resident has climbed steadily in every single state since 2008. Watch the map as the years advance: it grows more uniform, not less. This is one gap that has genuinely closed.

Migration & Diversity

Foreigners Prefer to Live in the West

Foreign nationals make up a rising share of Germany's population, and that rise accelerated after 2015. But the East has never caught up to the West. Berlin is the one clear exception.

Migration & Diversity

Asylum Seekers: The Syrian Civil War Effect

Asylum seekers registered per 1,000 residents jumped from about 4 in 2014 to over 15 in 2015, the year the Syrian civil war drove Europe's largest refugee movement in decades, then fell back toward 4 to 5 within a few years as arrivals slowed and cases were processed.

Faith

Belief Is Declining. The East Started Lower.

Church membership has fallen in every state since 2001, from Saarland's 87% down to 67%, from Baden-Württemberg's 74% down to 55%. But the five eastern states started the century already under 36%, a legacy of decades of state atheism, and have simply kept falling from there, several now under 20%.

Community

Sports Club Membership

West German states run anywhere from 22% to 37% of residents in an organized sports club, a Sportverein, often the default local institution. Every eastern state sits under 19%. That's not a temporary gap closing over time, it's barely moved since 2018, a genuinely different associational culture.

Volunteering

Baden-Württemberg, the volunteering capital

46.1% of Baden-Württemberg's population does some form of formal volunteer work, the highest of any state in the 2019 survey, the only year this measure exists. Saxony sits lowest, at 34.9%.

Germany is Not One

Some gaps closed. Others never moved.

Health access converged. Life satisfaction and life expectancy were never that far apart to begin with. But church membership, sports club culture, who is old or young where, and who lives where at all, still trace the same line drawn in 1990.
