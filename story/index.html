The Rise of the AfD
2013 – 2025

Twelve years, four federal elections, and a handful of state elections turned a party that missed parliament by a fraction of a point into Germany's second-largest political force. Scroll to watch it happen, region by region.

↓ Scroll to begin
2013

A rounding error

The AfD contests its first Bundestag election. Nationally it wins 4.7% of the vote — just short of the 5% threshold needed to enter parliament at all. In most of the country, it barely registers.

2017

Through the door

AfD clears 12.6% nationally and enters the Bundestag for the first time. It's already the single strongest party in Saxony — an early sign of the regional pattern that will come to define the next decade.

2021

A pause, not a retreat

Nationally, AfD's vote share actually dips slightly to 10.3%. But look closer: in Saxony and Thuringia it has just become the leading party outright for the first time — growth that a single national number hides completely.

2024 · Thuringia

The first state

AfD wins the Thuringia state election outright — clear of the CDU. It's the first time since 1945 that a German state election has been won by a party the domestic intelligence service classifies as confirmed right-wing extremist.

2024 · Saxony & Brandenburg

A close second, twice

Days later, AfD finishes a strong second in both neighboring states — officially narrowly held off by the CDU in Saxony and the SPD in Brandenburg. In this project's own municipality-average view (shown here), the rural-leaning arithmetic actually puts AfD ahead in both — a reminder that how you average a vote can matter almost as much as the vote itself.

2025

Second, nationally

Back at the federal level: AfD roughly doubles its 2021 result to 20.8%, becoming Germany's second-largest party overall — and, for the first time, the leading party in every one of the five eastern states at once.

12 years

One direction

4.7% → 12.6% → 10.3% → 20.8%. The map behind this text has shown every step of it. Explore the full, interactive versions to scrub through any year yourself, or compare this against unemployment, wages, and migration in the demographic tools.
